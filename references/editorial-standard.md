# Editorial Packet Standard

## Output Goal

The packet should feel like something a thoughtful teacher would hand out before
a serious shiur. It should be clean enough to print, comfortable enough to read
on an iPad, and restrained enough that the text stays primary.

## Layout

- Use a title page only for long packets. For one to four pages, start directly
  with the title and sources.
- Use a compact header:
  - title
  - subtitle or session label
  - source range
- Present bilingual material in two columns on desktop/print:
  - Hebrew on the right
  - English on the left
- Collapse to one column for small screens.
- Keep paragraph numbers visible but quiet.
- Use print-efficient page-break rules:
  - avoid excessive white space over preserving every source block intact
  - allow long source blocks to split naturally across pages
  - allow natural page flow for long paragraphs

## Typography

- Use system serif for source text where it improves dignity and readability.
- Use a clean system sans for metadata, labels, and footers.
- Hebrew needs generous line height and right alignment.
- Keep white space appropriate, not luxurious. These packets are meant to print.
- Avoid decorative fonts.
- Avoid tiny footnotes unless the source itself requires them.

## Tone

- No hype language.
- No generic inspirational framing.
- If adding a context note, keep it concrete:
  - what text
  - what span
  - what the reader should be ready to notice

- Pure white background for screen and print.
- Black or near-black text, with one quiet gray for secondary metadata.
- No warm paper fill, tinted page background, or accent color by default.
- Thin gray rules, not heavy boxes.
- No ornamental borders.
- No stock religious imagery.

## Verification

Before sending:

- Hebrew direction is correct.
- English and Hebrew ranges match.
- Page breaks do not orphan headings.
- The PDF has no private file paths, draft labels, or agent notes.
- The source citation is clear enough for someone to reopen the text.
